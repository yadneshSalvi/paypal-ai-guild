# Collection notes for the sample

This exhibition is a fictional museum setting around twelve real archival photographs. Harbor Street is not a substitute name for Detroit, Port Huron, Gloucester, Boston, New York or an unidentified island. The harbor map is an illustrated chapter navigator; it does not document historical geography.

The sequence is thematic, with one explicitly limited chronology. It begins with the working edge of a port, looks at passenger departures and fish-handling work, compares kinds of vessels and different waterfronts, makes room for an unidentified place, then explains how catalogue dates and sources are retained. Interpretive invitations to look are not presented as witnessed historical events.

The photographs show Great Lakes and Atlantic waterfronts. They do not establish a common voyage, a shared quay or a single community. No names, occupations beyond the catalogue subject, personal histories, destinations or working conditions are invented for the people pictured. Approximate dates and date ranges retain their original qualifiers. The lighthouse's island stays unnamed. A possible location for the unnamed steamer stays explicitly uncertain.

The comparison chapter pairs Boston and Detroit as distinct records, not as before-and-after restoration. The vessel room does not imply that all four ships were present together. The New York image is digital frame `4a10172`, one of ten negatives in the catalogue record, and is not described as a complete panorama.

The visual treatment is resizing and WebP compression from archival TIFF scans. No AI restoration, colourisation, generative fill, removal of people or invented historical detail was used. Some editorial layouts crop images; the source drawer and portable source register retain each complete frame.

All original titles, native contributor roles, dates, catalogue notes, later attributed summaries, rights advisories and file fingerprints are available from the source notes. For the twelve primary records and the measured verification report, see `docs/evidence/media/README.md`. For multilingual terminology, see `glossary.json`. Neither document is evidence of human fluent review.
